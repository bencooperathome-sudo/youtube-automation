This jellyfish can hit reset on aging and live again.
Meet Turritopsis dohrnii, the so-called immortal jellyfish found worldwide.
When stressed by injury, starvation, or age, it does something radical.
The adult medusa transforms back into a baby polyp, rewriting tissues.
Cells change identity in a process called transdifferentiation, like natural reprogramming.
From the polyp stage, it clones new adults and restarts the cycle.
Genes linked to DNA repair and stem-cell pathways stay unusually active.
It measures only a few millimeters across, making it hard to study.
Despite the nickname, predators, disease, and accidents can still kill it.
Scientists think its reset tricks could inspire anti-aging therapies.
